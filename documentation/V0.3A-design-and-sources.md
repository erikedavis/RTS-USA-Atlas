# V0.3A: city hierarchy and display rules

The Atlas uses established cartographic principles rather than claiming to duplicate proprietary consumer-map algorithms. The numerical thresholds and density rules below are our own implementation and remain adjustable.

## Research and decisions

- [Google Maps: marker collision management](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/javascript/examples/marker-collision-management) documents required markers, optional markers, and hiding lower-priority overlaps. Atlas prioritizes selected places and nationally important cities, then admits smaller places when room remains.
- [Google Earth: Regions and level of detail](https://developers.google.com/kml/documentation/regions) bases detail on visibility and projected screen size. Atlas uses projected pixel spacing and label rectangles, with more detail as the map expands.
- [Apple MapKit: display priority](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/mapkit/mkannotationview/displaypriority) provides annotation visibility priorities. [MapKit JS clustering](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/mapkitjs/clustering-annotations) addresses crowded annotations. Atlas adopts priority and collision handling; it retains actual city locations instead of replacing cities with numbered cluster bubbles.
- [Waze editor: interface and controls](https://www.waze.com/discuss/t/map-editor-interface-and-controls/377960) describes hiding some feature classes at wider zooms to avoid clutter. [Waze community guidance on city updates](https://www.waze.com/discuss/t/timeline-of-updating-process/378880) discusses city labels changing with viewport and zoom. These are editor/community references, not documentation of Waze's proprietary consumer-label algorithm.
- [Total War: Rome II campaign manual](https://r2enc.totalwar.com/en/manual/single-player/0012_enc_page_campaign_play/index.html) documents selecting settlements and zooming from region information. Atlas preserves direct city/name selection, state abbreviation zoom, and the details panel.
- [Victoria 3 developer diary 74](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/victoria-3/news/dev-diary-74-ux-improvements) describes filtering information by relevance and significance. Atlas uses that hierarchy principle; this diary does not disclose a city-label placement formula.
- [MapLibre style specification](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-style-spec/layers/) provides geographic symbols, text anchors, offsets, and data-driven styling. Atlas keeps labels and points inside the map renderer so they remain geographically attached during zoom and pan.

## Implemented policy

All 32,629 bundled Census places are eligible. Capitals, state-largest cities, and Boston, Philadelphia, Miami, Washington, Los Angeles and Las Vegas receive additional priority. The national view protects the top 20 cities plus those landmarks and uses the V0.2F label size and regular weight. V0.3A.1 measures the surrounding concentration of cities before applying any size distinction. In crowded metro views large cities shrink more slowly than small places as you zoom. Ordinary regional labels use the standard size. Small places near a larger city appear at closer zooms, with names deferred to hover if space is still tight.

Selection always protects the selected city's point and name, including when selection came from a polygon or search. A lone admitted town is named. Local screen cells nominate their most important place, letting small places appear in sparse areas. A zoom-dependent population threshold admits additional candidates, but local winners are not subject to that threshold. Pixel spacing, a viewport-sized budget and measured label rectangles suppress crowding. Labels try eight sides of their point; displaced required labels use leader lines. Previous placements receive a small preference to reduce unnecessary rearrangement.

Hover uses ordinary geographic text in bold at 115% of its usual size and a glowing point. Leaving both point and label starts one 500 ms timer; further blank mouse movement does not postpone it indefinitely. Returning cancels the timer. Moving the map or deselecting clears hover immediately.

State abbreviations are 20% larger than V0.2F and retain their 15% hover enlargement. City labels reserve room for them. A visible state can retain an edge abbreviation when its usual anchor is off screen. Counties remain explicitly opt-in at every zoom. Phone layout and touch interactions are the next development pass; desktop hover is not presented as an iPhone solution.

Puerto Rico's inset uses a larger display scale and an independent abbreviation beneath the shoreline. The inset heading and frame reserve their space from outside city labels.

## V0.3A.2 refinements

At regional scale, a population threshold keeps small towns out and gives major state cities more prominence. During the metro overview (zoom 7.55 to 9.15), the map emphasizes existing Census MSA shapes and names. City symbols dim and reveal their names on hover. After the metro overview, local town names return. The key explains four population tiers and the separate capital asterisk.

Names on the map select a city in place. Its population symbol jumps to it. State badge placement tests space inside the visible state shape. Leader lines are reserved for small-state badges in the national view, and RI and DC use a nearby badge where necessary. State zoom uses the full shape and a small viewport margin.

## V0.3A.3 refinements

The map now places city and state names after camera movement settles. Existing labels stay anchored during the gesture. Clicking state land opens the state panel at the current zoom, while the state abbreviation still fits its full outline.

The seven population shapes and cutoffs come from the population key in the [HSR Georgia reference map](https://national-hsr-control-center.erik-davis-2826.chatgpt.site/api/maps/835ce9fa-f990-4b3c-b7de-ca1cc72d650a.svg). Atlas uses city populations for these symbols and a single color. Capital asterisks are separate from the population shapes.

The geographic label layer starts with the Great Lakes, the Upper Peninsula and Long Island. Each name has a fixed anchor and ID. The labels yield to other map text, and Long Island fades at close zoom. The geographic layer currently has no selection action.

3,222 county equivalents have July 1, 2025 estimates; 13 island equivalents use April 1, 2020 counts, visibly dated. Where available, largest incorporated city/town is ranked by residents within the county, with both county-part residents and whole-city population clearly distinguished. Elsewhere, the largest Census place whose representative point lies within the county is shown with that method stated. Four county equivalents contain no bundled Census place; no largest city is invented. Census places include incorporated cities/towns and census-designated places, not every informal named settlement.
